CrossOff Privacy Policy

Effective 9 November 2026

CrossOff is a personal productivity app for iPhone and Android that helps you block distracting apps, finish what you planned, and stay accountable with people you trust. This policy explains what data CrossOff handles, what it deliberately cannot see, what the people you link with can see, who else is involved, and what you can ask of us. It is written to be read, not skimmed past.

The short version: your tasks and progress sync to your own account; which apps you block and how long you use them never leave your phone — on iPhone, Apple's design means we cannot see it even if we wanted to, and on Android it stays in the app's own on-device storage; the friends you link with see what you choose to share plus the accountability signals this page lists; a few small records are readable by any signed-in account, and this page names them; we show no ads and sell nothing to anyone.

Who we are

CrossOff is made and run by Rinchen Lama, a sole trader trading as CrossOff in Australia. We are responsible for everything this page describes — the data controller, in UK and EU terms. Write to us at support@crossoff.io; our postal address is available on request.

What we collect

  • Account: when you sign in with Apple, Google, or an email address and password, we receive your name (where the sign-in provides one), your email address, and a user ID. Sign in with Apple lets you hide your email. To confirm an email address we send a one-time code through SendGrid, an email delivery service, which receives your address for that purpose and to deliver the reports you send us (below). While a code is valid we store a hashed form of it; the record also holds the address it went to and when codes were sent, and is removed about a day later. Passwords are handled by Firebase Authentication and are never visible to us. "Keep me posted" is switched on when you set up CrossOff; if you leave it on, we keep your email address on a list to send you occasional CrossOff news and tips — a few a year, never daily, always with an unsubscribe link. This is our own newsletter and carries no third-party advertising. Turn it off at any time in Settings › Account (CrossOff news and tips by email) or with the link in any such email; if you unsubscribe we keep only a note not to write to you again, and we delete the entry when you delete your account. If you signed in with Apple and hid your email, the newsletter goes to your Apple relay address and Apple forwards it. When you sign in, the app records on your account that you accepted these terms and this policy — which versions, when, and which sign-in button you used.
  • Things you create: tasks, lists, actions, schedules, the steps inside a task, XP and completion history, the coins you earn and what you unlock in the Store, your settings (your theme and character, reminders, sharing switches) and the answers you gave when you set up CrossOff (the areas you picked, the band of screen time you said you have). These sync to your account so your devices stay in step. A daily safety copy is kept for seven days, and you can make one yourself or restore one from Settings.
  • When you crossed things off: your completion history includes the time of each completion. The app uses those times on your phone to time reminders around when you usually do things — for example, when you have skipped a day, a nudge shortly after your usual time if the task is still open. This is worked out on your device each time and is not stored or sent anywhere; you can turn these reminders off in Settings › Notifications.
  • Friend connections: who you have linked with and when, anyone you have blocked, friend requests and invite codes (both carry your name and, until a later update, your email address), your @username, an email-to-account lookup entry so friends can find you, the approval requests you send or answer, the pacts and challenges you are in, a push-notification address for each of your devices with that device's time zone (for quiet hours) and your switches for friends' notifications, the day your app was last opened and whether it was an iPhone or Android phone (a date, never a time of day) and, while "Show when I'm online" is on, when you were last in the app.
  • Subscription status: whether your CrossOff Pro subscription is active, via Apple and RevenueCat (below). We never see your payment details — Apple keeps those.
  • Diagnostics: when CrossOff crashes, or hits an error it handles but wants us to know about, a report goes to Sentry, our error-reporting service, with the technical details of the failure, your account's internal ID, an 8-character code for this installation, the app version and update you were running, your subscription status, basic facts about your phone (model, system version, whether it is a tablet, text size, whether a screen reader or Reduce Motion is on — so we can reproduce layout bugs — language and time zone), a record that the app was opened, and a short trail of recent taps, console lines and network calls (which part of the app, which server — never the words you typed, as far as our scrubbing can tell, and never what the server answered). Reports never include your tasks, lists, limits, websites or friends. Crash reporting has no switch in the app, because without it we cannot find the bug that stopped CrossOff working for you; email us if you would rather we never saw yours, and we will remove your account's reports and stop collecting them. We also record a small number of in-app events (for example, that the paywall was viewed, or that setup was completed) in our database and send them to PostHog (see Usage statistics below). None of this is sold or used for advertising.
  • Reports you send us: if you choose to send a report from Settings › Report a problem, what you write is emailed to our support inbox, with the email address on your account as the reply address so we can answer you, together with technical details about the app on your phone: your account's ID, when it was made and how you sign in, the installation code, the version, your phone's system version, language and time zone, whether your data has synced, your subscription status, how many reminders are set, your Screen Control settings — on/off switches (the 18+ filter's among them), counts, your focus-hour times, which limits fired today and which a relax day doubled, and the internal codes of the monitors armed (never the names you gave them) — your coin balance and last three payouts, your theme and text size, and how many tasks, lists and friends you have. A copy goes to Sentry (below) so it sits beside any crash from the same account. It never includes what your tasks or lists say, the names you give your limits, websites, which apps you use, or who your friends are. The screen summarises what goes before you send. To help us fix problems, the app also keeps a short on-device log of its own recent actions (for example, "a sync finished"), which travels with a report or a crash report and is deleted when you sign out.

Sensitive things you might write

What you put in a task, habit or list is yours to choose, and some of it may be about your health, beliefs or private life. We store and sync it only to show it back to you and to the friends you share it with; we never read it, analyse it, or send it to our analytics or crash-reporting services. By typing it you agree to us storing it for that purpose. If you would rather not, write it in a way only you understand.

What we deliberately cannot see

On iPhone

CrossOff's blocking is built on Apple's Screen Time technology (Family Controls). Apple designed it so that:

  • We never learn which apps you block. When you pick apps in the system picker, CrossOff receives anonymous tokens that only mean something on your device. The names and icons of your chosen apps are never visible to us and never leave your iPhone.
  • Your app usage stays on your device. Time measurements run inside Apple's sealed system extensions on the phone itself. CrossOff's servers receive none of it. The Screen Time report inside the app is drawn by iOS in a sealed view the app cannot read.

On Android

  • Blocking runs inside the app on your phone. It uses Android's Usage access and "display over other apps" permissions to notice which app is open and to count the minutes you spend in each app (the on-device report covers every app you can launch; the limits only count the apps you chose). While blocking is on, a persistent notification says so and names the app you are in. Which apps you chose, today's minutes, and any unlock grants are stored on the phone only and are never sent to us. The shield never covers CrossOff itself, your phone app, messages, home screen, the system Settings or your alarm clock, as your phone reports them. The app may ask you to exempt it from battery optimisation so the blocking keeps running; that is a system setting, not data we collect.
  • The web filter is an on-device DNS filter. While it is on, your phone's website lookups go to Cloudflare's public resolver (1.1.1.1, or 1.1.1.3 for the family filter) instead of your network's own, so adult sites and the sites you listed can be blocked. It uses an encrypted connection where it can; when that is unreachable it falls back to ordinary DNS to the same resolver, and when neither answers, lookups fail rather than go anywhere else. CrossOff does not log, store or transmit those lookups, and no other traffic passes through it. The filter's settings and your blocked list stay on the phone. Android allows one VPN at a time, so another VPN app connecting switches the filter off; the app tells you when that happens.

On both

  • Vault labels and limit names are local. If you name a Vault entry or a limit, that name stays on your device only. When you ask a friend to open your Vault, they are told you want to open "your Vault" — never what is inside it.
  • Your web filter is nobody else's business. The 18+ content filter, the websites you block, and whether the filter is on, off, in your Vault or paused are never shared with friends and are not synced to your account. The only time any of it reaches us is as on/off switches and counts inside a problem report you choose to send — never the websites. A friend asked to approve a change is told only that it is your Vault or a protected setting, never what it is.
  • Focus Mode and relax days. A friend asked to approve ending Focus Mode early is told that, and nothing about what you were focusing on. A finished Focus session is saved to your account as a Focus entry in your History, with the task's text. That a Focus session or a relax day happened is a usage event (below); a relax day's details ride only a problem report you choose to send.

What your friends can see

Accountability works by sharing — but only what you switch on, plus the signals listed here, and you can see exactly what that is in the app:

  • Your display name, @username, level, and the character and backdrop you wear — not your email address. (Friends' lists made before November 2026 carried it; the app removes it the next time either phone opens.)
  • Lists you explicitly share, and the sections of your list you share from a friend's Sharing screen. Sharing one list, or one Goals tab, lets that friend's app read your whole lists or goals record; their app shows them only the parts you shared. A friend you let edit a shared list can change it.
  • The Friends board: if you join it, friends on it see how many things you crossed off each day this week, your level, XP and current streak. What you crossed off — each completion and its time — stays behind a curtain you open one friend at a time. The apps show it only to a friend who has opened theirs for you; the data itself is shared the moment you open yours. You can hide a single completion from friends from your History.
  • Requests you send them — for unlock time, a settings change, opening your Vault, or ending Focus Mode early — with the minutes asked, never app names or Vault contents.
  • The day you last opened CrossOff ("hasn't opened the app in 3 days"): the app shows it to the friends you chose as Vault approvers, and shows you the same about them. The record itself is readable by any signed-in account (see Other accounts below).
  • Whether a PIN they hold for you was used — an honesty signal, part of the deal.
  • Whether you're using the app right now, or roughly when you last did ("Last online 3 hours ago"). Only the people on your friends list can see this. It is on when you install CrossOff; turn it off on the My Friends page (Show when I'm online) — when it's off, nothing is shared.
  • The progress you share with a friend includes your clock's offset from UTC, so their app knows when your week ends and can pay the Monday coins on time. It's a clock setting, not a location — and only the friends you share progress with can see it.
  • If you switch off CrossOff's blocking from your phone's settings while blocking is on — Screen Time access on iPhone, Usage access or the overlay permission on Android — linked friends get one notification saying so, and one when it is back (or a single one afterwards, if CrossOff only notices once it is already back on). While it is off, their Friends tab shows how long it has been off and may offer to nudge you to turn it back on; you can pause those nudges, and that choice is copied to your friends' lists so their apps respect it. The fact and the length of the outage are recorded on your friends list (see Other accounts below). Nothing else about your Screen Control reaches them — and turning Screen Control off inside the app with Face ID or your passcode tells nobody.
  • If your subscription lapses and your blocking turns off, linked friends get one notification saying so.
  • Pacts and challenges: the people in a pact or challenge with you see your name and which days you did its actions, for as long as it lasts. In a group pact, the app shows members you have not added as friends only that the pact has one more member — but the pact's record, which every participant's app reads, holds every member's name, days and character. If the person who proposed it chose an open room, which every invitee is told before joining, everyone in it is shown everyone's name, days and character.
  • Pushes between friends are sent through our servers, which check that the two of you are connected before forwarding the push. The words of a friend's nudge, a shared list's name and a pact's text pass through Expo's push service and then Apple's or Google's to reach the phone. We keep, for about two hours, when you sent pushes and to whom, and when they reached you — never the words — to limit how many a person can send. Your switches for friends' notifications are read by our server when it forwards a push; a friend's phone never reads them, though a friend who tries to nudge you is told when that kind is switched off.

Linking is a two-way agreement

When you and a friend link, each of you accepts the list above for the other. Every friend can be asked to approve your unlock, settings, disable, unlink and Focus requests, and a friend can name you as a Vault approver without asking first; you can decline or unlink at any time, and unlinking never opens anyone's apps. When you unlink, both PINs stop working, your shared views of each other are deleted, a pact between the two of you ends and a group pact one of you proposed marks the other as having left; a Vault they approved stays locked until you name another approver. Your friend's phone drops your board, online status and time zone the next time it refreshes, keeps any pact you were both in until that pact ends, and keeps the notifications it already received. We ask every user to treat what a friend shares as private; using CrossOff to monitor or control someone is against our terms.

Finding friends

Friends link by invite code, QR code, @username or email. A request by username or email has to be accepted by the other person; declining is quiet, and blocking someone sends them nothing (though see Other accounts below). Anyone signed in to CrossOff who knows your email address can find your account that way and send you a request; there is no switch for this yet, and declining costs you nothing. CrossOff can also suggest people who are friends with at least two of your friends: a suggestion shows their name, username, level and character, and which of your friends they share — nothing else. You can turn off appearing in other people's suggestions in Friends › My Friends.

Other accounts

A few small records are readable by any signed-in CrossOff account, not only your friends, because the app needs them to connect two people: your display name, @username, character, backdrop and level; the list of people you are linked with and when you linked them; whether you appear in suggestions; the people you have blocked; the Screen Control outage marks described above; the day you last opened the app and which kind of phone it was; and the scores from the retired solo games, for accounts that played them. Your email address, tasks, lists, history and settings are never among them. Nothing in the app shows another person these records about you; a determined person with their own software could read them. We intend to narrow this, and this page will say when we have.

Usage statistics

To see which parts of CrossOff are used and how long they take, the app sends usage events to PostHog (PostHog Inc., EU Cloud — the data is held in the EU), a service provider processing on our behalf: the screen opened and the seconds spent on it, which of a fixed set of buttons and actions were used, the answer to the notification and Screen Time permission prompts, that a Focus session or a relax day happened, that the app was opened or updated, the setup answers (the areas you picked, the screen-time band), and the subscription plan. Each event is tied to your account id and carries the app version, update, operating system, device model, screen size, language and time zone. Never what you write — no action, habit, list, friend or Vault names, no email, no notes. Usage statistics are switched on when you install CrossOff. PostHog works out your country and roughly which city from your IP address and then discards the address; the place is kept with the event. Turn it off any time in Settings › Account › Share usage statistics; it stops from that phone at once, so turn it off on each phone you use. Usage events are kept for 12 months.

Services we rely on

  • Apple — sign-in, payments and subscriptions on iPhone, the Screen Time system itself, and the delivery of notifications to iPhones (APNs). Apple acts under its own privacy policy for these.
  • Google — Sign in with Google, and the delivery of notifications to Android phones (Firebase Cloud Messaging), under Google's own privacy policy.
  • Google Firebase — authentication, the database where your synced data lives (hosted in Sydney, Australia), and our small server functions: five run in the United States (the email sign-in codes, the mailer for the reports you send us, the relay for pushes between friends, the newsletter list's export) and two database triggers run beside the database in Australia.
  • SendGrid (Twilio, United States) — delivers the one-time code when you confirm an email address, and the reports you send from Settings › Report a problem. Receives your email address, and a report's contents, for those purposes only.
  • Namecheap Private Email (United States) — hosts our support inbox: holds the reports you send us and our replies.
  • RevenueCat (United States) — subscription receipt validation. Receives your user ID and purchase receipts; never your payment details.
  • Expo (United States) — delivery of most push notifications (a friend's words pass through it on the way to Apple or Google; the Lock Screen approval card goes from our server straight to Apple) and of app updates; receives your device's push address.
  • Sentry (United States) — crash reporting and the reports you send from Settings › Report a problem.
  • PostHog (EU Cloud) — the usage statistics described above. Receives your account id and those events; never what you write.
  • Cloudflare — on Android only, and only while the web filter is on: the public DNS resolver your website lookups are sent to (see above).
  • GitHub Pages — hosts this website and the shared content the app downloads (reminder messages, character and backdrop art). Like any web host it sees the ordinary details of a request, such as your IP address; it receives nothing about your account.

Firebase, SendGrid, Namecheap, RevenueCat, Expo, Sentry and PostHog process data only on our instructions, under written agreements, and may not use it for their own purposes or advertising. Apple and Google act as controllers in their own right for sign-in, payments and push delivery. Cloudflare's resolver and GitHub Pages are public services we point the app at; they handle your IP address under their own policies and we have no agreement with them about your data.

Where your data goes

We are in Australia, and your synced data is stored by Google Firebase in Australia; our server functions run in the United States. Sentry, RevenueCat, Expo, SendGrid, Namecheap and GitHub are in the United States, and PostHog is a US company that holds our data in the EU. If you are in the UK or the EU, your data therefore leaves your region: we rely on the UK–US Data Bridge and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where a company is certified, and otherwise on the standard contractual clauses (with the UK Addendum) in our agreements with them and with Google. Email us for a copy of the safeguards.

Why we are allowed to

For people in the UK and the EU, their data-protection law asks us to say which legal basis covers each use of your data; we say it for everyone. We process it because:

  • it is needed to provide CrossOff to you (our contract with you): your account, syncing and safety copies, your friend links and the signals this page lists, approval requests, pacts and challenges, your subscription status, pushes between friends, and answering a report you send us;
  • we have a legitimate interest in keeping CrossOff working and safe: crash and error reports and the on-device log that travels with them, the short-lived push and report records that stop flooding, and the checks that two accounts are really connected, and showing your friends when you were last in the app (on by default, with its switch on the My Friends page). You can object to any of these by emailing us; crash reporting has no in-app switch, for the reason given above;
  • you have agreed: the newsletter ("Keep me posted"), usage statistics ("Share usage statistics"), joining the Friends board, sharing a list or your progress, linking with a friend, and anything else you switch on. You can withdraw each one in the place you switched it on, and withdrawing does not undo what was done before.

Nothing CrossOff decides automatically has a legal or similarly serious effect on you: reminders, nudges and the Monday coin settle are the only automated decisions, and all of them can be switched off or ignored.

How long we keep things

Your account and synced data: until you delete the account. Safety copies: seven days, then they are replaced. Email sign-in codes: the record goes about a day after the code expires. Problem reports: the emailed copy stays in our support inbox for twelve months after we last wrote to you; the Sentry copy, and crash reports, for 90 days. Usage statistics: 12 months. Push and report send records: stamped to expire after about two hours and a day. The newsletter list: until you unsubscribe or delete your account; after an unsubscribe we keep only a "do not write" note. Online status: deleted the moment you switch it off; the last-opened day is overwritten each time you open the app and deleted with your account. We do not yet delete accounts for inactivity; delete yours from the app whenever you like.

How we protect it

Everything between the app and our services travels encrypted and is stored encrypted by Google (the Android web filter's fallback to plain DNS, above, is the one exception). Access rules on the database let each account read only its own data and the shares this page lists, and write only its own data plus the requests and feed rows it sends to others; our own access is through Google's console with two-factor sign-in. We never store passwords. On your phone, the app's data sits inside the protections your phone's own lock gives it. If a security problem ever puts your data at risk we will tell you in the app or by email as soon as we can, and report it to the Australian Information Commissioner and, where the law requires it, to the UK or EU authorities within 72 hours.

Deleting your data

Settings › Account › Delete account removes your account, your synced data and its safety copies, your @username and the email lookup entry, your push addresses, last-opened day and online status, your entry on your friends' lists and your own list of friends and blocks, your feed, the friend requests you sent and received, your newsletter record, your sharing records, and the invite code made in that session. Lists you shared stop being shared. A few things it does not reach by itself, and we remove by hand when you email us: earlier invite codes (they stop working after seven days, but the record, with your name and email, stays until someone tries the code or we remove it), approval requests you sent (your name, the type and the minutes), the rows you wrote into friends' feeds, the in-app events in our database and at PostHog, and your RevenueCat record. You leave any pact or challenge the next time a member's app syncs. Crash reports and problem reports already sent expire at Sentry after 90 days. Device-local Screen Control configuration is removed when you delete the app; a phone backup you restore may bring it back with the app. If anything ever seems left behind, email us and we will remove it by hand.

Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of the data we hold about you, have mistakes corrected (most things you can edit in the app), have it deleted (Settings › Account › Delete account, or ask us), receive the data you gave us in a machine-readable file (we email it to you as a JSON file), restrict or object to how we use it, and withdraw any consent you gave. Email support@crossoff.io; we answer within one month. If you are unhappy with how we handle your data you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au); if you live in the UK, to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk); if you live in the EU, to your own data-protection authority. We do not sell personal information and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, as US state privacy laws define those words, and we never have.

Children

CrossOff is for people aged 13 and over — 16 and over where your country's law sets that age for agreeing to online services without a parent. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone younger; if you think a child has an account, email us and we will delete it. CrossOff is not a parental-control product: a parent can be a friend who holds your key, but the account, the data and the choices are the user's own.

This website

crossoff.io is hosted by GitHub Pages, which sees the ordinary details of a request (your IP address, browser type and the page asked for) and keeps them in its own logs under GitHub's privacy statement. We set no cookies, load no third-party fonts or scripts, and run no analytics on the site.

Changes

When we change this policy in a way that affects what we collect, why, who sees it or how long we keep it, the app shows you a notice the next time you open it. Smaller changes just update the date above. Earlier versions are available on request.
