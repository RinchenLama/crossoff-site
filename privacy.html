CrossOff Privacy Policy

Effective 8 November 2026

CrossOff is a personal productivity app for iPhone and Android that helps you block distracting apps, finish what you planned, and stay accountable with people you trust. This policy explains what data CrossOff handles, what it deliberately cannot see, what the people you link with can see, and who else is involved. It is written to be read, not skimmed past.

The short version: your tasks and progress sync to your own account; which apps you block and how long you use them never leave your phone — on iPhone, Apple's design means we cannot see it even if we wanted to, and on Android it stays in the app's own on-device storage; the friends you link with see what you choose to share plus a few accountability signals this page lists; we show no ads and sell nothing to anyone.

What we collect

  • Account: when you sign in with Apple, Google, or an email address and password, we receive your name (where the sign-in provides one), your email address, and a user ID. Sign in with Apple lets you hide your email. To confirm an email address we send a one-time code through SendGrid, an email delivery service, which receives your address for that purpose only; while the code is valid we store only a hashed form of it. Passwords are handled by Firebase Authentication and are never visible to us. If you keep "Keep me posted" switched on when you set up CrossOff, we keep your email address on a list to send you occasional CrossOff news and tips — a few a year. Turn it off at any time in Settings › Account, or with the unsubscribe link in any such email; we delete the entry when you delete your account.
  • Things you create: tasks, lists, actions, schedules, the steps inside a task, XP and completion history, the coins you earn and what you unlock in the Store, and your settings (your theme and character, reminders, sharing switches). These sync to your account so your devices stay in step. A daily safety copy is kept, and you can make one yourself or restore one from Settings.
  • When you crossed things off: your completion history includes the time of each completion. The app uses those times on your phone to time reminders around when you usually do things — for example, when you have skipped a day, a nudge shortly after your usual time if the task is still open. This is worked out on your device each time and is not stored or sent anywhere; you can turn these reminders off in Settings › Notifications.
  • Friend connections: who you have linked with and anyone you have blocked, friend requests, invite codes, your @username, an email-to-account lookup entry so friends can find you, the approval requests you send or answer, the pacts and challenges you are in, a push-notification address for your device together with your switches for friends' notifications, the day your app was last opened (a single date, never a time of day) and, while "Show when I'm online" is on, when you were last in the app.
  • Subscription status: whether your CrossOff Standard subscription is active, via Apple and RevenueCat (below). We never see your payment details — Apple keeps those.
  • Diagnostics: when CrossOff crashes or hits an error it cannot recover from, a report goes to Sentry, our error-reporting service, with the technical details of the failure, your account's internal ID, the app version you were running, and basic facts about your phone (model, system version, text size, language). We also record a small number of in-app events (for example, that the paywall was viewed, or that setup was completed) so we can understand what is working (see Usage statistics below). Reports never include your tasks, lists, limits, websites or friends, and none of this is sold or used for advertising.
  • Reports you send us: if you choose to send a report from Settings › Report a problem, what you write is emailed to our support inbox, with the email address on your account as the reply address so we can answer you, together with technical details about the app on your phone: your account's ID, when it was made and how you sign in, a short code for this phone, the version, your phone's system version, language and time zone, whether your data has synced, your subscription status, how many reminders are set, your Screen Control settings as on/off switches and counts, and how many tasks, lists and friends you have. A copy goes to Sentry (below) so it sits beside any crash from the same account. It never includes what your tasks or lists say, the names you give your limits, websites, which apps you use, or who your friends are. The screen shows the full list before you send. To help us fix problems, the app also keeps a short on-device log of its own recent actions (for example, "a sync finished"), which travels with a report or a crash report and is deleted when you sign out.

What we deliberately cannot see

On iPhone

CrossOff's blocking is built on Apple's Screen Time technology (Family Controls). Apple designed it so that:

  • We never learn which apps you block. When you pick apps in the system picker, CrossOff receives anonymous tokens that only mean something on your device. The names and icons of your chosen apps are never visible to us and never leave your iPhone.
  • Your app usage stays on your device. Time measurements run inside Apple's sealed system extensions on the phone itself. CrossOff's servers receive none of it. The Screen Time report inside the app is drawn by iOS in a sealed view the app cannot read.

On Android

  • Blocking runs inside the app on your phone. It uses Android's Usage access and "display over other apps" permissions to notice when a blocked app opens and to count the minutes you spend in the apps you chose to limit. Which apps you chose, today's minutes, and any unlock grants are stored on the phone only and are never sent to us.
  • The web filter is an on-device DNS filter. While it is on, your phone's website lookups go to Cloudflare's public resolver (1.1.1.1, or 1.1.1.3 for the family filter) over an encrypted connection instead of your network's own, so adult sites and the sites you listed can be blocked. CrossOff does not log, store or transmit those lookups, and no other traffic passes through it. The filter's settings and your blocked list stay on the phone.

On both

  • Vault labels are local. If you name a Vault entry, that name stays on your device only. When you ask a friend to open your Vault, they are told you want to open "your Vault" — never what is inside it.
  • Your web filter is nobody else's business. The 18+ content filter, the websites you block, and whether the filter is on, off, in your Vault or paused are never shared with friends and are not synced to your account. The only time any of it reaches us is as on/off switches and counts inside a problem report you choose to send — never the websites. A friend you ask to open the Vault is told it is "a protected setting", never what it is.
  • Focus Mode, relax days and limit names stay on your device. A friend asked to approve ending Focus Mode early is told that, and nothing about what you were focusing on.

What your friends can see

Accountability works by sharing — but only what you switch on, plus the signals listed here, and you can see exactly what that is in the app:

  • Your display name, @username, level, and the character and backdrop you wear — not your email address. (Friends' lists made before November 2026 carried it; the app removes it the next time either phone opens.)
  • Lists you explicitly share, and the sections of your list you share from a friend's Sharing screen. A friend you let edit a shared list can change it.
  • The Friends board: if you join it, friends on it see how many things you crossed off each day this week, your level and XP. What you crossed off stays behind a curtain you open one friend at a time — and they see it only if they have opened theirs for you. You can hide a single completion from friends from your History.
  • Requests you send them — for unlock time, a settings change, opening your Vault, or ending Focus Mode early — with the minutes asked, never app names or Vault contents. If you have chosen a friend as a Vault approver, they can see which day you last opened CrossOff ("hasn't opened the app in 3 days"), and you can see the same about them.
  • Whether a PIN they hold for you was used — an honesty signal, part of the deal.
  • Whether you're using the app right now, or roughly when you last did ("Last online 3 hours ago"). Only the people on your friends list can see this, and you can turn it off on the My Friends page (Show when I'm online) — when it's off, nothing is shared.
  • The progress you share with a friend includes which time zone your phone is in, so their app knows when your week ends and can pay the Monday coins on time. It's a time zone, not a location — and only the friends you share progress with can see it.
  • If you switch off CrossOff's blocking from your phone's settings while blocking is on — Screen Time access on iPhone, Usage access or the overlay permission on Android — linked friends get one notification saying so, and one when it is back. Nothing else about your Screen Control reaches them.
  • If your subscription lapses and your blocking turns off, linked friends get one notification saying so. Accountability you can switch off silently isn't accountability.
  • Pacts and challenges: the people in a pact or challenge with you see your name and which days you did its actions, for as long as it lasts. In a group pact, members you have not added as friends see only that the pact has one more member — unless the person who proposed it chose an open room, which every invitee is told before joining; then everyone in it sees everyone's name, days and character.
  • Your notification switches for friends' activity (nudges and challenges, shared lists) are shared with your friends' phones so they can respect them — a friend's app skips a push you turned off. Nothing else about your settings is shared this way.
  • Pushes between friends are sent through our servers, which check that the two of you are connected before forwarding the push to the notification service. We keep, for one hour, when you sent pushes and to whom — never the words — to limit how many a person can send.

Finding friends

Friends link by invite code, QR code, @username or email. A request by username or email has to be accepted by the other person; declining is quiet, and blocking someone is never disclosed to them. CrossOff can also suggest people who are friends with at least two of your friends: a suggestion shows their name, username and character, and which of your friends they share — nothing else. You can turn off appearing in other people's suggestions in Friends › My Friends.

Usage statistics

To see which parts of CrossOff are used and how long they take, the app sends usage events to PostHog (PostHog Inc., EU Cloud — the data is held in the EU), a service provider processing on our behalf: the screen opened and the seconds spent on it, which of a fixed set of buttons and actions were used, the answer to the notification and Screen Time permission prompts, and the subscription plan. Each event is tied to your account id and carries the app version, update, operating system and device type. Never what you write — no action, habit, list, friend or Vault names, no email, no notes. Your IP address reaches PostHog with each request and is discarded, not stored. Turn it off any time in Settings › Account › Share usage statistics; it stops from that phone at once. Usage events are kept for 12 months.

Services we rely on

  • Apple — sign-in, payments and subscriptions on iPhone, and the Screen Time system itself.
  • Google — sign in with Google.
  • Google Firebase — authentication, the database where your synced data lives, and the small server functions that send and check email sign-in codes.
  • SendGrid — delivers the one-time code when you confirm an email address, and the reports you send from Settings › Report a problem. Receives your email address, and a report's contents, for those purposes only.
  • RevenueCat — subscription receipt validation. Receives your user ID and purchase receipts; never your payment details.
  • Expo — delivery of push notifications and of app updates.
  • Sentry — crash reporting and the reports you send from Settings › Report a problem.
  • PostHog — the usage statistics described above. Receives your account id and those events; never what you write.
  • Cloudflare — on Android only, and only while the web filter is on: the DNS resolver your website lookups are sent to (see above).
  • GitHub Pages — hosts the shared content the app downloads (reminder messages, character and backdrop art). Like any web host it sees the ordinary details of a request, such as your IP address; it receives nothing about your account.

Each receives only what it needs to do its job. None of them may use your data for their own advertising.

Deleting your data

Settings › Account › Delete Account removes your account, your synced data and its safety copies, your invite codes and friend requests, your push address, last-opened day and online status, your entry on your friends' lists, and the usage statistics tied to your account id. Lists you shared stop being shared, and you drop out of any pacts or challenges you are in. Device-local Screen Control configuration is removed when you delete the app. Crash reports and problem reports already sent are kept by our error-reporting service for a limited time and then expire. If anything ever seems left behind, email us and we will remove it by hand.

Children

CrossOff is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from them.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the app will say so — we won't rely on you re-reading this page. The effective date above always reflects the current version.
