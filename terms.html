Blocking, and its limits

On iPhone, app blocking uses Apple's Screen Time system. That system is Apple's, not ours, and it is not perfect: iOS may occasionally deliver a scheduled block or unblock late or not at all, and usage measurements are approximate. Phone calls and Emergency SOS are never blocked — Apple enforces that at the system level.

On Android, the block screen is drawn by CrossOff itself over the apps you chose, using the Usage access and "display over other apps" permissions you grant, and a persistent notification shows while blocking is on. The shield never covers CrossOff itself, your phone app, messages, home screen, the system Settings or your alarm clock, as your phone reports them — but Android is not iOS: don't choose the apps you rely on to call for help, and keep another way to be reached. The app may ask you to exempt it from battery optimisation so blocking keeps running; some phones stop it anyway. Reminders on Android may arrive a few minutes late.

CrossOff is a tool to support your intentions, not a guarantee that an app can never be opened. The Vault's time-lock, a friend's PIN and the approval requests are honour systems backed by software; software has bugs, phones restart, and you can always remove the app.

Please don't rely on CrossOff for anything safety critical. If you need to be reachable, don't block the way people reach you, and keep another way to be contacted.

What your friends are told. Accountability only works if the people you chose hear when blocking stops. So, if you switch off CrossOff's blocking from your phone's settings while it is on — Screen Time access on iPhone, Usage access or the overlay permission on Android — your linked friends get one notification saying so, and one when it is back, and their Friends tab shows how long it has been off. If your iPhone subscription lapses and blocking turns off, they get one notification saying so, and your blocks lift rather than leaving your phone restricted. Turning Screen Control off inside the app with Face ID or your passcode tells nobody. The privacy policy lists every signal a friend receives.

The web filter (Android)

The web filter is an on-device DNS filter that sends your phone's website lookups to Cloudflare's public resolver so adult sites, the sites you list and unsafe search results can be blocked. It blocks most adult sites, not all; it forces safe search on the major search engines; it is user-initiated and you can switch it off at any time (a friend's approval is asked for when you've linked one, but Android's own VPN settings always work). Another VPN app connecting switches the filter off. Cloudflare's resolver is a public service under Cloudflare's own terms.

Accountability friends

Linking is by mutual consent and either side can unlink at any time. Linking means: every friend can be asked to approve your unlock, settings, disable, unlink and Focus requests; a friend can name you as a Vault approver without asking first, and you can decline or unlink; a friend may hold a PIN for you, and your use of it is reported to them. Only link with people you actually trust — you control what's shared and can change it at any time. A friend who approves or declines a request is doing you a favour, not taking on a duty: approvers and PIN holders are not responsible for what you do with or without your phone, and neither are we. Treat what a friend shares with you as private. Don't use CrossOff to coerce, monitor or control another person; a person who wants out can always unlink, block you, or delete their account.

Pacts and challenges: the people in a pact or challenge with you see your name and which days you did its actions; in an open-room group pact everyone in it sees everyone, and every invitee is told so before joining.
